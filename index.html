Our mission

Kehillah Labs builds open software that Jewish communities own together, and advises all spectrums of Jewish leadership on AI.

Open, always

MIT-licensed. Any congregation can run it, inspect it and improve it.

Made for Jewish life

The Hebrew calendar, yahrzeits, religious school and synagogue finances come built in.

Built to last

A nonprofit home sustained by hosting income and grants, so it never depends on one person.

The problem

Synagogues run on software they rent and don’t control

Recurring fees

Closed commercial systems charge every year, a real burden for small congregations.

Locked-in data

Member records, yahrzeits and giving history live in a vendor’s system, on the vendor’s terms.

Patchwork tools

Many offices juggle spreadsheets, QuickBooks, email tools, Zoom and an aging database.

Flagship project

ShulNET 2.0 runs the whole synagogue office

One open platform for the work every congregation does, designed around Jewish communal life rather than adapted from generic nonprofit software.

How we sustain the work

Free code, paid convenience, grant-funded growth

Open source, free forever

MIT license. Congregations with volunteer IT can self-host at lower cost.

Hosted service

Earned income from congregations that want it run for them, priced to recover costs.

Grants

Support from Jewish foundations and federations that fund communal infrastructure.

Below-market pricing

Fees only recover costs. Gifts and grants cover the rest, in the spirit of Sefaria’s open model.

The founder

Brian Kresge, MBA

The problem is personal.

As president of a small Conservative synagogue whose budget has been in retreat for seven years, Brian watched commercial software price his congregation out, and saw how many congregations have a reluctant relationship with their software vendor.

Deep engineering experience.

Thirty years of large-scale software engineering. Brian started ShulNET in 2020 and rebuilt it from the ground up in 2025. He designs and trains AI models in healthcare diagnostics and population health.

Equally deep Jewish leadership.

Two decades in synagogue executive leadership, an endorsed lay leader throughout a 30-year military career, and co-founder of Jews in Green. Brian is due to receive smicha from the Pluralistic Rabbinical Seminary in December 2026.
